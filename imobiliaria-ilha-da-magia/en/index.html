How a long-term rental works

Fiador, caução or seguro-fiança.

It is the first question any tenant in Brazil asks, and there is not one line about it on the current site. A plain, factual explanation, with no fee figures.

⛹

Fiador

A person who takes on responsibility for the contract alongside you, usually by proving income and presenting a mortgage-free property of their own. It is the most traditional route, and the one that depends most on who you know in the city. [CONFIRM]

▣

Caução

A cash deposit given as security at the start of the contract and returned at the end if there are no arrears or damage. The tenancy law caps the amount, and it sits in a savings account tied to the contract. [CONFIRM]

⚖

Seguro-fiança

An insurance policy taken out with an insurer that replaces the guarantor. You pay the premium and the insurer guarantees the contract to the landlord — useful for someone who has just arrived in the city and has no fiador. [CONFIRM]

[CONFIRM which guarantee types he accepts and whether he works with a partner insurer.] No fee, premium or percentage appears here — those change by contract and by insurer, and a wrong number in this section becomes a complaint.

And a rule of this build: this site never collects a CPF, income documents or guarantor details through a form. That moves to a secure channel after first contact.
