The drop list — and what it is not

The list that says when the rail is restocked contains only people who asked to be on it, through its own form, with its own unticked box. It is not built from people who messaged asking the price of a dress, nor from people who reserved a piece, nor from people who bought once. Asking about a piece is not signing up to a list.

Leaving takes one word: reply “stop” in the conversation and it is done, with no reason required and no retention attempt. Consent is revocable at any time (LGPD art. 8, § 5), and revoking it affects no purchase already made.

Reserving a piece

Reserving over WhatsApp feels like a conversation, but when the piece goes by post it becomes a distance sale: a name, a phone number, a CPF for the invoice and a delivery address. Those data are used to invoice and to send, and nothing else. How long they are kept [CONFIRM] — invoices carry their own statutory period and cannot be deleted on request.

When someone asks what size they wear

If you send your measurements to find out whether a piece will fit, that is personal data about your body. It is used to answer your question and nothing else: it does not go on a list, it does not become a segment, and it is not stored for later. [CONFIRM how long conversations stay on the device]

Consignors

Someone leaving pieces hands over two things at once: personal data and property. The policy covers the contact details and the record of the pieces received; the consignment contract covers the split, the period, what happens to what does not sell and who is liable for loss or damage. Photographs of pieces belong to the shop. No customer photograph is published without written image-rights consent — and that includes Instagram reposts: being tagged is not a licence. If a consigned piece appears in a photograph its previous owner took, her permission is needed too. [CONFIRM]
