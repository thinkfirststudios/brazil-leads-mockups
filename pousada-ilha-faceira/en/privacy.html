⚠️ The TripAdvisor ranking claim

Their homepage carries, hand-written and undated: “Our guesthouse is No. 1 of 321 in Florianópolis, according to TripAdvisor reviews.” Rankings move. A position fixed in the code that quietly ages stops being social proof and becomes advertising that has stopped being true — exposure under art. 37 of the Brazilian consumer code, not merely an embarrassment.

This mockup asserts no position at all. The module exists, but it renders the line only beside a visible date, and that date is a [CONFIRM] until the value is pulled live. The ten review handles and dates shown are the ones already on their homepage; each review’s text comes from TripAdvisor through the integration, and none has been written or rewritten here.

The TripAdvisor widget is a third-party tracker

Pulling the reviews live is the right thing to do, and it has a privacy cost: the widget hands the visitor’s IP and an identifier to TripAdvisor. So it sits in the marketing category of the consent gate and does not load before consent. In this build it is not even wired up. The map in the location section is an SVG drawn here, precisely so Google is not called without consent.

What this site stores

One thing: faceira.consent.v1, your cookie choice, in localStorage on this device. It reaches no server and disappears when you clear site data.

The forms

There are two: the availability enquiry and the contact form. Both compose a text message and hand it to WhatsApp, in the language of the page you are on. Neither checks a calendar, neither writes to a server and neither confirms a booking. While the number is unconfirmed they send nowhere at all: they show the notice above. Dates render as DD/MM.
