For the owner who is not here

You bought a house on this island and you are inside it four weeks a year

The rest of the time it sits on its own. This is not a camera catalogue — it is the four things that actually go wrong in an empty coastal house, and what the system does about each one.

The gate was left open

Someone left and did not shut it. With a camera on the gate and remote access you can see it, and with access control you can shut it.

Somebody arrived — and was expected

A caretaker, a cleaner, a gardener, a delivery. Knowing who came in and when is half the value of the system; being able to grant that access and then revoke it is the other half.

The power went out and the system went with it

A recorder and a router on an ordinary socket go down with the street. The answer is a UPS and an internet path that holds — and it is in the infrastructure section, not hidden.

Salt air ate the hardware

Salt film on the lens, corrosion on the bracket, an oxidised connector. It is the most common failure on this island and the one periodic maintenance exists to catch.

Remote access

How this actually works

It is the section no competitor here writes, and it is the four questions an absentee owner actually has.

What you can see

Live view and recorded playback. They are different things: live is now, recording is going back. How long footage is kept is a design decision and also a legal one [CONFIRM].

Who else can see it

A caretaker, a property manager, a rental agency. Everyone with access is a user, and what matters as much as granting is being able to revoke when that person moves on.

What reaches you

A motion notification is useful when the house should be empty and becomes noise when it is not. No response time and no detection rate is published here [CONFIRM].

If the power or the internet drops

The honest answer: without a UPS the recorder and the router go off and you are blind. With a UPS and a redundant internet path, they do not. Exactly what changes depends on what gets specified [CONFIRM].

Scope

Installation, not monitoring

This site describes installation and configuration. We install the system and set you up to look at it. We are not a monitoring company: there is no control room watching your feeds and no response dispatch. That is not a technical limitation — monitoring is regulated private security activity, authorised and supervised by the Policia Federal, and anyone doing it needs federal authorisation.

If Security Floripa also monitors, this whole section changes — and the authorisation has to be documented before a single word about it goes live. No authorisation reference has been invented or paraphrased in this mockup. [CONFIRM: install only, or monitoring too?]

Infrastructure

Power and internet — the part nobody sells

It is what separates an installer from a box-fitter. A camera system on an unprotected circuit with a domestic router is offline exactly when it matters.

  • A UPS on the recorder AND the router, not just the recorder
  • Surge protection — relevant on this island
  • Structured cabling, labelled, with slack for maintenance
  • Sealing and corrosion resistance in salt air
  • An internet path that holds, and what happens when it does not

Privacy and LGPD

A camera records personal data — and that is your problem, not the installer's

No competitor on this island says a word about it, and for a foreign owner already nervous about local rules they do not understand, hearing it explained is worth more than being the cheapest.

Who is responsible

The property owner is generally the data controller. The installer's role needs to be defined in the contract, not assumed [CONFIRM how they handle this today].

Signage that the area is monitored

Visible signage is not decoration: people entering need to know. That covers visitors, deliveries, cleaners and guests.

Not filming what is not yours

A camera pointed at the neighbour's house, the street or a shared access road captures data about people who have nothing to do with your security. Framing is a design decision.

How long footage is kept

Retention is a choice, not an accident, and keeping everything forever is not the safer option [CONFIRM the period they configure].

Who has access, and how it is removed

Granting access is easy; revoking it is what almost nobody documents. Every user should be traceable and removable.

Domestic staff and rented properties

Monitoring people who work in your house is a sensitive subject; a camera inside a short-let property is a serious problem, not a detail. Both need discussing before installation.

FAQ

What people ask before hiring

Can I watch from another country?

Yes — that is exactly what the system is set up for. What changes from country to country is the quality of your own connection, not the system.

What if the internet at the house drops?

Without internet there is no remote access, but with a UPS the local recording continues and you catch up afterwards. Without a UPS the recorder goes down with it. [CONFIRM what they specify]

How long is footage kept?

It depends on the design — and it is an LGPD decision as much as a disk one. No period is published here [CONFIRM].

Can I give the caretaker access and take it away later?

That is the right question. Granting is easy; what matters is that the system allows revoking and that you know how. [CONFIRM how this is handed over]

Do I have to tell people entering the house there are cameras?

Signage that an area is monitored is good practice and, depending on the case, an obligation. It covers visitors, deliveries, cleaners and guests.

Are cameras allowed inside a short-let property?

A camera in the interior of a rented space is a serious problem, not a detail. That conversation happens before installation, with the right contract wording [CONFIRM the practice].

Who looks after maintenance while I am away?

That is the maintenance section on this page, and what is included and how often [CONFIRM].

What is the warranty?

The statutory warranty is 90 days for durable goods and services (CDC art. 26, II). Any longer period is contractual and comes in writing (art. 50). The installation warranty and the manufacturer warranty are separate. [CONFIRM]

Do you monitor?

No — this site describes installation and configuration. Monitoring is regulated private security activity authorised and supervised by the Policia Federal. [CONFIRM]
