Guesthouses and holiday properties

Series work for guest rooms, and the wear of a short-let interior

A different buyer and a different cycle. A guesthouse room is used by strangers, every day, and its joinery is a durability problem before it is an aesthetic one.

[CONFIRM whether the workshop serves this segment] The lead sheet mentions “high-ticket pieces for regional properties”, which on this island usually means second homes and guesthouses — but that is our reading, not their statement.

Repetition with tolerance

Twelve identical rooms are twelve different openings. A series piece needs its adjustment clearance designed in, not improvised on the day.

Spare parts

A door, a drawer and hardware held as spares are worth more than a sophisticated finish. Break it in high season and you swap it rather than rebuild it.

Reception and common areas

The reception counter is the one piece every guest touches. It is treated as commercial furniture, not domestic furniture.

How we work with other trades

What a joinery workshop does not do

Saying this out loud prevents the most common scope argument in the trade, which is finding out on delivery day that the countertop was never included.

Stone countertops belong to the marmoraria

Granite, marble, quartz and a carved basin are another trade and another measurement — taken after the joinery is installed and levelled.

Glass and mirror belong to the vidraceiro

A glass door, a closet mirror and a glass shelf come through a specific supplier, with the measurement taken on site.

Upholstery belongs to the tapeceiro

An upholstered bedhead, a bench with a seat pad and a window cushion combine a joinery structure with an upholsterer’s covering.
