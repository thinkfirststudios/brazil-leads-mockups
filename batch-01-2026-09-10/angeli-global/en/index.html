    Investing in Brazil as a non-resident

    What actually happens, in plain English

    This replaces the returns calculator as the site's primary content magnet — and it is genuinely better, because it answers the question a foreign buyer actually loses sleep over.

    01

    CPF — the Brazilian taxpayer number

    A CPF is required to hold title. Residency is not required to buy property in Brazil. The CPF is obtained through the Receita Federal, in most cases via a Brazilian consulate. [CONFIRM current process with counsel]

    02

    Registering inbound funds with the Banco Central

    This is the step that determines whether sale proceeds can later be remitted out of Brazil. Funds entering the country for the acquisition should be registered so that the capital is recognised on the way back out. Getting this wrong at purchase is discovered at exit, when it is expensive to fix. [CONFIRM current registration regime]

    03

    Ownership structure — individual, SPE or holding

    Each route carries different liability, succession and reporting consequences. The comparison below states them qualitatively. We make no tax-saving claim and no numeric comparison.

    04

    Title due diligence

    Matrícula and its full chain, certidões on the vendor and the property, ônus reais, and any condominium or IPTU arrears that attach to the asset rather than the seller.

    05

    Closing — escritura, cartório, ITBI

    The deed is executed before a notary and registered at the competent cartório. ITBI, the municipal transfer tax, is assessed by the municipality and is payable on transfer. [CONFIRM rates and timing per municipality with counsel]

    06

    Exit — capital gains and remittance out

    Disposal triggers a Brazilian capital-gains assessment, and remitting proceeds abroad depends on the registration made at step 2. Treaty position depends on the investor's own jurisdiction. [CONFIRM with tax counsel in both jurisdictions]
