Your Brazil timeline

Four stages, in the order they actually happen.

Not a service menu. Most people arrive not knowing what to ask for — so this is the list of things that need doing, in sequence, with a person attached to each one.

  • ✓ Apostille your documents IN YOUR HOME COUNTRY, before you fly.
    A document issued abroad is generally apostilled where it was issued — not in Brazil. Birth and marriage certificates, degrees, criminal record checks. This is the mistake that costs people months, and it is the one thing to get right before boarding.
  • ✓ Work out which documents you will actually be asked for.
    The list depends on your situation and on what you intend to do here.
  • ✓ Get translations planned, not improvised.
    Official use in Brazil generally requires a sworn translator — a tradutor público juramentado. Coordinating that in advance is cheaper than doing it twice.

FAQ

The questions people actually arrive with.

Do I need to be in Brazil to start?

No — and for the apostille step you should specifically not wait. Most of what needs doing before you fly happens in your home country.

What is an apostille, and do I need one?

It is a certificate under the Hague Convention that makes a public document from one member country valid in another. Whether you need one depends on the document and on what you are using it for. A document issued abroad is generally apostilled in the country that issued it — not here.
