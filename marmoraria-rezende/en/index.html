Materials

Quartz first — and the reason is an honest one.

Choosing between marble and quartz for a kitchen is choosing between beauty and maintenance. Saying that plainly is worth more than any superlative.

    Quartz

    An engineered, non-porous surface. It is the most predictable thing you can put in a kitchen: it barely stains, the colour and pattern are consistent slab to slab, and it needs no sealing. It does not like direct heat — a hot pan goes on a trivet.

Warranty

The statutory warranty is 90 days for durable goods and services, under art. 26, II of the Brazilian Consumer Code. Anything longer must be given in writing, under art. 50. The stone warranty and the installation warranty are separate things, and engineered quartz usually carries a manufacturer warranty of its own with its own conditions — which are not described here because we have not read them. Safety in cutting

The conversation nobody in this market is having.

Engineered quartz carries a very high proportion of crystalline silica and is the material most associated with accelerated silicosis worldwide. The hazard is dry cutting.

Nothing has been published here about Rezende’s practice. In Brazil this sits under NR-15 Anexo 12 (exposure limits for free crystalline silica) and NR-01/NR-09 (PGR, a mandatory employer programme). A workshop that cuts wet, with extraction and PPE, has a real and defensible story to tell — and it is exactly the professionalism a specification-led buyer recognises.
