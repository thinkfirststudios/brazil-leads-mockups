Children’s data: the rule is different

The children’s jiu-jitsu class means a minor’s name, age and details in the records. LGPD art. 14 treats that separately from everything else and requires three things:

  • the processing must be in the child’s best interest;
  • it needs specific, highlighted consent from a parent or legal guardian — a generic tick-box at the foot of a form does not do;
  • a child’s participation cannot be conditioned on handing over more data than is necessary.

That is why the children’s enrolment form on this site starts with the legal guardian, keeps the consent separate from everything else, and will not proceed without it. The child does not go onto a marketing list, and the guardian can withdraw consent at any time. [CONFIRM where that consent is recorded today and who has access to the children’s class records]

Images of children

Photos and video of a kids’ class are the content that circulates most in this trade and the content that demands the most care. A minor’s image requires specific written permission from the guardian, separate from the enrolment contract, stating where the image may appear and for how long — and that permission can be withdrawn, which in practice means knowing which post each child appears in.

No image on this site shows a child. Every photo is stock, labelled as a placeholder, and shows adults or empty spaces. [CONFIRM the studio’s image policy]

Five activities, five councils, no registration published

Physical training, physiotherapy, chiropractic, acupuncture and cryotherapy do not answer to the same professional council, and each brings its own duty of confidentiality and its own record-retention period. A physiotherapy record, for instance, is not deleted because the client asked: a regulatory period applies.
