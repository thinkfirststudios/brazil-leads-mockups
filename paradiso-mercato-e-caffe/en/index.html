Why this site opens with two doors

Nobody knows what you are

What the research found. The source spreadsheet carried no description at all — not one word. The only confirmed channel is the Instagram profile @paradisomercatoecaffe, which serves a login wall to any server-side request: no bio, no menu, no hours, no address, no phone. Everything we think we know comes from translating the handle: mercato (market) + e caffè (and café).
The possible readings, all of them live. (a) an alimentari of Italian imports — pasta, oil, wine, cheese, cured meat — with a coffee counter; (b) a café and light-lunch spot with a small retail shelf as a secondary line; (c) a full trattoria using “mercato e caffè” as branding rather than as a description; (d) a specialty roaster with an Italian pantry range. [CONFIRM which]

If a paid researcher holding the handle cannot say what the business is, a customer in Florianópolis holding a phone certainly cannot. That ambiguity is the product problem, and the website is the fix. One screen — the one above, with two doors — resolves in three seconds what the Instagram grid has never resolved.

Second, and it is worth money: the mercato inventory is a search asset you are throwing away. Every category on your shelves — massa italiana Florianópolis, azeite importado Floripa, queijos italianos Florianópolis — is a search somebody is running today and finding a competitor for. A retail category page ranks; an Instagram post does not.

                Allergens & labelling

                Two different duties in one shop

                This block matters more here than in an ordinary restaurant, because a mercato-caffè has prepared food on one side and packaged shelf goods on the other, and the rules are not the same.

                • Prepared food — RDC 26/2015

                  Mandatory per-item declaration: wheat (gluten), milk, egg, soy, tree nuts, peanuts, fish and crustaceans as applicable, plus a cross-contact statement. [CONFIRM] per item, with the kitchen.

                • Gluten — Lei 10.674/2003

                  “CONTAINS GLUTEN” or “GLUTEN FREE” is mandatory, and in a place with pasta and bread the cross-contact risk is high enough to deserve its own sentence. [CONFIRM]

                • Packaged goods

                  The label governs. This site does not paraphrase a label, does not invent an ingredient list and makes no nutritional claim. Once there are products, the instruction is to photograph or link the label, never to rewrite it.
