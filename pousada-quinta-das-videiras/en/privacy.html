⚠️ Three things this build did not publish, and why

This is the part of this policy that matters most on this lead. Three elements of the current site were not carried into the mockup, and each has a concrete reason.

1. The “10 best hotels in Brazil” ranking

Their homepage says: “We are among the 10 best hotels in Brazil.” With no awarding body and no year. It appears nowhere in this build. An unsourced ranking is advertising that cannot be substantiated — exposure under art. 37 of the Brazilian consumer code — and even if the source exists, an undated ranking ages and becomes false on its own. [CONFIRM the publication and the year, or drop it entirely]

2. The four 9,4/10 scores

The homepage displays four badges — 9,4/10 FANTÁSTICO, 9,4/10 EXCEPCIONAL, 9,4/10 FANTÁSTICO, 9,4/10 EXCELENTE — and no platform logo rendered, so where any of the numbers come from is unknown. No score has been printed in this mockup. The section exists, with marked slots for the platform, and the guidance is to pull live rather than hardcode: a fixed score ages exactly like a ranking. [CONFIRM which platforms]

3. The signed testimonials

The homepage carries two testimonials attributed to named people, each with a stated profession. Republishing a person’s name, their profession and a sentence attributed to them is a privacy and risk question, not merely an editorial one. LGPD treats a name as personal data, and attributing a statement to an identified person needs a legal basis — in practice, documented permission.

So the testimonial module in this build degrades gracefully: until written permission exists it shows initials only and marks the text as to be confirmed. There is a switch at the top of script.js that controls this, and it is off. [CONFIRM written permission from each guest quoted]

There are also two pull-quotes on the homepage with no visible attribution at all. Before republishing them, where they came from needs establishing. [CONFIRM the source and date of both]

⚠️ No WhatsApp link exists on the current site

In Brazil WhatsApp is usually the primary channel — and there is not one wa.me link on their site. The commercial number carries a mobile prefix and probably answers there, but probably is not confirmed, and this mockup does not build a link from a guess: if the line does not answer on WhatsApp, the message simply never arrives. The buttons stay disabled and explain that. Both published phone numbers work now, as calls.

What this site stores

One thing: quinta.consent.v1, your cookie choice, in localStorage on this device. It reaches no server and disappears when you clear site data. The map in the location section is an SVG drawn here, not an embedded map, precisely so Google is not called without consent.

The forms

There are four: the availability enquiry, weddings and events, a table at the Bistrô and the newsletter. All compose a message and hand it to WhatsApp; none writes to a server and none confirms a booking. Dates render as DD/MM.

Two notes. First, weddings and room bookings have separate forms, because they are different sales with different timescales, values and questions — today they share one path. Second, the newsletter has its own unbundled consent, in a checkbox of its own, as LGPD requires — never folded into another form’s acceptance.

Accessibility

Worth recording as a finding: the current Wix-built site is leaking an accessibility helper string as visible navigation text — “Use tab to navigate through the menu items” appeared on both pages fetched. Not serious in itself, but it is a symptom: worth testing real keyboard navigation, with a visible focus state on every control.

What this site does not claim

No rate, unpublished floor area, rating, ranking, award, founding year, CNPJ or CADASTUR number appears without being marked [CONFIRM]. The suite measurements and descriptions are transcribed from their own /suites page, word for word — including where the Luxo category gave no description and Premium gave no floor area: the card says so rather than filling in.

The three building elevations are drawings, not photographs, and their alt text says so. There is no honest stock image of Casarão Lisboa, of a stained-glass Solarium whose frame came from Vietnam, or of Casa Porto — and putting the wrong building there would be worse than drawing one. Every photograph is stock and none depicts the hotel, the suites or the team. No image shows children.
