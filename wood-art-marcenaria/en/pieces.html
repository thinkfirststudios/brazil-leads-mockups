None of these pieces is Wood Art Marcenaria’s. They are stock photographs marked as placeholders, put here so the layout renders complete. Timber and finishes

What you are paying for, said before you sign

The species and the finish are the product. Each card below carries the category and one honest line about how that material ages, how it behaves near the sea and how it is repaired.

PLACEHOLDER-madeira-estocada.jpg
Timber

Solid timber

It moves with humidity: it expands and contracts with the season, and the design has to let that movement happen. It can be sanded back and restored as many times as needed. [CONFIRM species and origin documentation]

PLACEHOLDER-veio-reto.jpg
Timber

Natural veneer

Real grain over a stable substrate: the figure of timber without the movement of solid wood. Repair is more delicate, because the veneer is thin. [CONFIRM thickness and supplier]

PLACEHOLDER-superficie-clara.jpg
Timber

Faced MDF

Stable, flat and predictable — the base of most fitted joinery. Its weak point is the edge and contact with water: sealing and edging decide how long the piece lasts. [CONFIRM range and facing]

PLACEHOLDER-tabuas-empilhadas.jpg
Timber

Plywood

Cross-laminated plies: it holds a screw better than MDF and tolerates more moisture, with the exposed edge some pieces use deliberately. [CONFIRM type and glue class]

PLACEHOLDER-veio-rustico.jpg
Finish

Varnish

A film over the timber: it protects well and resists liquids, but when it is scratched the repair is to the whole film, not to the spot. [CONFIRM product and number of coats]

PLACEHOLDER-parede-madeira.jpg
Finish

Oil

It penetrates rather than covers: the timber keeps its feel and a repair stays local. In exchange it asks for periodic maintenance. [CONFIRM reapplication interval]

PLACEHOLDER-ripado-escuro.jpg
Finish

Lacquer

A closed colour and an even surface, over panel rather than over visible grain. Touching it up means the piece goes back to the spray booth. [CONFIRM whether there is an in-house spray booth]

PLACEHOLDER-formao-cavaco.jpg
Finish

Wax and stain

Wax gives a low sheen and a dry touch; stain changes the colour without hiding the grain. Both are the easiest finishes to touch up at home. [CONFIRM whether they are offered]

No timber species appears anywhere on this site, and that is deliberate. Native Brazilian timber requires legal-origin documentation — the DOF, Documento de Origem Florestal, under IBAMA, plus state controls. Publishing a species name without it is a factual and a regulatory risk at the same time. [CONFIRM species, supplier and origin documentation]

Process

Five stages, and the drawing comes before the cut

A commissioned piece genuinely has five stages. Compressing them into three makes the most important one — the drawing being approved — invisible.

1

Briefing

Whatever reference you have, the measurements of the opening, and what the piece has to hold or take.

2

Drawing and detailing

The drawing: dimensions, materials, hardware and how each joint resolves. [CONFIRM whether drawing is in-house or comes from the architect]

3

Drawing approved

Nothing is cut before you approve it. This is where a change is cheap — after the cut, it is not.

4

Production in the studio

Cutting, machining, assembly and finishing. [CONFIRM typical lead time from drawing to delivery]

5

Delivery and installation

Transport, levelling and adjustment on site. [CONFIRM whether installation is in-house]
