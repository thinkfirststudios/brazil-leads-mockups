The four words

Organic, vegan, cruelty free, natural

These are the four words that sell this category — and the four that cause the most trouble when they appear with nothing behind them.

Organic

Depends on certification by an accredited body, product by product. It is not a marketing adjective.

Evidence: [CONFIRM certificate]

Vegan

No animal-derived ingredient. A vegan mark is issued by a certifier and applies per product, not per shop.

Evidence: [CONFIRM certificate]

Cruelty free

Not tested on animals. Several Brazilian states ban animal testing for cosmetics, which changes what the mark signifies here.

Evidence: [CONFIRM certificate]

Natural

The slipperiest of the four: it has no single legal definition. Which is why this site describes what is inside instead of repeating the adjective.

Evidence: [CONFIRM certificate]

None of these four words appears anywhere on this site as a settled claim. A March 2020 article records the shop saying that “most products are organic”, that they are “99% vegan” and that “all are cruelty free”. That is five and a half years old, it did not come from the shop to us, and a catalogue percentage moves every time a new supplier arrives. Republishing it as today’s fact would be misleading advertising if it were wrong — CDC arts. 30 and 37.
The right move is the opposite, and it sells better: say which lines are certified, by whom and since when. A named mark with a year beats “99% vegan” in any reading.

Brands

Forty-odd brands, and not one of them written here

The curation is the product. It is also the most valuable search asset this shop owns — and the only one that cannot be published without permission.

Why a brands page is worth so much. People looking for natural cosmetics almost never search “natural cosmetics shop”: they search a brand name plus the city. Every brand the shop carries is a doorway that does not currently exist. A 2020 article spoke of more than 40 brands, Brazilian and imported from Germany, Italy, the USA, Lithuania and Sweden. [CONFIRM the current list]
No brand name has been written on this page, and that is deliberate. A third party’s mark is a third party’s property: publishing the list needs written permission from each supplier, and several distribution agreements have a clause about exactly this. It is the first thing to ask for and it usually comes back quickly, because it suits both sides. [CONFIRM permission brand by brand]
