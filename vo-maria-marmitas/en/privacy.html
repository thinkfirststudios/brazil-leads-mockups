A subscription holds a home address and a routine

A one-off delivery uses an address once. A weekly plan holds where you live, which day you are home to receive and how often — and holds it for as long as the plan runs. Together those three fields describe a routine, which is why access is limited to the day’s route and they are not exported to a marketing tool, a shared spreadsheet or a messaging group.

Who runs the route, whether delivery is in-house or by app, and what the courier can see of the order [CONFIRM]. If delivery is outsourced, the partner is a processor and needs a contract with a data-protection clause [CONFIRM who it is].

Allergens: the label is an obligation, not a courtesy

ANVISA RDC 26/2015 requires food allergens to be declared, and Lei 10.674/2003 requires the statement “contém glúten” or “não contém glúten”. That applies to the packaging, not only to the website. What is printed on the marmita label today [CONFIRM].

Corporate accounts: third-party data

When an office subscribes for its team it hands over other people’s names and often their dietary restrictions. Each employee remains a data subject: they can ask us directly for access, correction and deletion, and the buying company does not waive those rights on their behalf. The company also needs its own legal basis for having collected that information.

The right to withdraw — and its limit

CDC art. 49 gives seven days to withdraw from a purchase made away from the premises. Perishable food already cooked and delivered is a case where that right runs into the nature of the product, and what the kitchen will accept — cancel before the cutoff, swap, credit the following week — has to be written down before payment.
