The bowl builder stores data on this device

This is the most significant collection on the site, which is why it sits at the top. The builder uses your browser’s localStorage for three things:

  • opoke.bowl.v1 — the bowl you saved with the “Save my bowl” button: the chosen options, the quantity and the notes.
  • opoke.track — whether you were looking at the Poke line or the Sushi line.
  • opoke.consent.v1 — your cookie choice, so you are not asked again.

None of it leaves your device. There is no server receiving it, no account, no user identifier, and none of these values is transmitted anywhere. They stay in your browser until you delete them — and the builder itself has a “Delete saved bowl” button that removes the record immediately. Clearing site data in your browser has the same effect.

Allergens: why the builder carries them into the message

ANVISA RDC 26/2015 makes allergen declaration mandatory, and a build-your-own flow that drops the allergen at the summary is a real risk, not a detail. So every option in the builder carries its allergen list and that list travels with the message composed for WhatsApp.

One important, deliberate caveat: because Opoke! publishes no ingredients at all, the allergens shown today are the categories possible for that type of step, not the composition of a real item, and each option says so. The forms

There are four — the bowl builder, the postcode check, the catering request and the WhatsApp list. All work the same way: they compose a text message and open WhatsApp. None of them sends anything on its own, none writes to a server, and you see the message before you tap send. From the moment you send it, the conversation is handled by WhatsApp under Meta’s policy, outside this site’s control. [CONFIRM how long order conversations are kept].

The postcode check deserves its own sentence: it does not query any third-party service and does not pretend to know the delivery radius. It asks, on your behalf, over WhatsApp.

Price, fee and minimum order

No price appears in this build because none is published. When they do, the consumer code and Decreto 5.903/2006 require the price to be total and unambiguous and require the delivery fee and the minimum order to appear before checkout, never revealed only at the end. The builder’s total is designed for exactly that: it adds everything up in the open, with no hidden extra. Today it shows [CONFIRM] rather than a number, on purpose.

Cookies
