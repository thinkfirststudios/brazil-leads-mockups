The domain on our lead sheet is not theirs

This lead’s record listed akilar.com.br as Wood Art’s website. That address belongs to AkiLar, a home-services listings portal based in Jaraguá do Sul that sells advertising plans to trades and whose own terms disclaim being a service provider. The strings “Wood Art” and “marcenaria” do not appear on that page.

If a Wood Art listing really does exist inside the portal, that has a data-protection consequence: enquiries arriving through it were collected by the portal, under the portal’s terms, and passed on. The portal answers for that consent, and the workshop ends up processing data it did not collect. This needs writing down before it becomes a question. Photos of your home and your architect’s drawings

A joinery estimate starts with you sending a photo of your room, the measurement of your wall and, often, your architect’s detailing. Those three stay restricted to whoever estimates and builds, and they do not go into a messaging group, a shared cloud folder or a sales presentation.

The architect’s file is a third party’s work. It exists to estimate and build your piece; it is not reused on another client and it is not republished. [CONFIRM the retention and disposal policy for design files]

A photo of the finished piece is a separate consent

In joinery the portfolio is the credibility, which makes the temptation to photograph everything strong. Photographing the installed piece inside a client’s home requires specific written permission — separate from the purchase contract, revocable, and stating where the photograph may appear. An internal technical record is one thing; posting on Instagram is another.

Estimates, warranty and timber origin

A prior written estimate is a consumer right under CDC art. 40; it itemises labour, materials and dates, and it binds for 10 days from receipt unless otherwise agreed. Nothing outside it can be charged without authorisation.

The statutory warranty is 90 days and splits into three: the piece, the installation and the hardware — the last being the manufacturer’s, with its own conditions. [CONFIRM the terms]

Timber origin. Native Brazilian timber requires legal-origin documentation — the DOF, under IBAMA, plus state controls. That is why no species is named anywhere on this site. [CONFIRM species, supplier, documentation and any FSC certification — never invent one]
