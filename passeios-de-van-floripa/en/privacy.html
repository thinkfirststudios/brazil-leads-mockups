Why the flight number deserves its own paragraph

On its own a flight number is not much. Alongside the arrival time, the passenger’s name and the hotel address — which is exactly the set a transfer booking collects — it places a specific person, in a specific place, at a specific hour. That set deserves a defined retention period, access limited to whoever is running the job, and disposal at the end. [CONFIRM the retention period, who has access and how it is disposed of].

The practical consequence for the operation: that information should not circulate in a shared WhatsApp group or sit indefinitely in the history of a personal handset.

The broken WhatsApp link

Their page carries two WhatsApp links. One is correct: wa.me/554899900608. The other is wa.me/48999900608, with no +55 country code — and that one fails on any foreign phone, which is much of the audience for an airport transfer business in Canasvieiras. Every link in this mockup uses the +55 form, and the link on the live site is worth fixing regardless of any contract.

The forms and what they do

There are three: the route picker, the corporate form and the contact form. All work the same way: they compose a text message and open WhatsApp with it ready, in the language of the page you are on. None writes to a server, none sends on its own, and none confirms a booking. You see the message before you tap send. From that point the conversation is handled by WhatsApp under Meta’s policy.

What this site stores

One thing: pvf.consent.v1, your cookie choice, in localStorage on this device. It reaches no server and disappears when you clear site data.

Nothing third-party loads before consent

No Google Analytics, no pixel, and no embedded map — the map in the contact section is an SVG drawn here, precisely so Google is not called without your consent. The photographs come from a stock image CDN. Analytics and marketing are off by default and “Reject all” carries the same weight as “Accept all”.
